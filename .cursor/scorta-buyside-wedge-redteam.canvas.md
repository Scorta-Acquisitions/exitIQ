# Kill file: “AI-native buy-side representation” is not the wedge

**Verdict:** The memo correctly executes co-brokerage. It then replaces that dead payment channel with a worse one. Buyer-paid advocacy plus lender referral is not a modification of the thesis. It is two businesses that cannot occupy the same 7(a) file, cannot be delivered by two founders at the modeled price, and cannot be paid by the customer the model needs. Week-11 collected revenue at contact with reality is **$3–8k**, not **$48k**. Do not staff the hybrid. The company is the **change-of-ownership file**, sold to people who already get paid when it funds.

---

## Part 1 — Systematic destruction

### 1. Co-brokerage and payment mechanics

**Fatal.** Already dead in the memo. Residual “call 10 brokers” work is still in the week-1 list and will waste the only scarce input (founder hours).

There is no MLS cooperation-and-compensation rule. IBBA “encourages” cooperation; it creates no right to a split. Listing engagement letters pay the listing broker. On a live listing that broker keeps 100%. Co-broke shows up on stale, thin, or unsellable inventory — which is adverse selection, not a GTM channel. The NAR settlement is not supporting evidence for a Main Street buyer-agent model. Residential is abandoning seller-paid buyer-agency **and** it had a touring gate that forced written buyer-rep agreements. Business brokerage has no equivalent gate. No signed buyer-rep, no tour, no deal. The analogy proves the opposite of what the memo needs: without a structural compulsion to hire you, first-time buyers do not.

**Not neutralized.** The memo “kills” co-broke and then keeps testing it. That is how dead hypotheses consume batches.

---

### 2. Buyer willingness and ability to pay

**Fatal for the $5–15k SKU. High-probability for retainers.**

Cash conservation is not a preference. It is the credit box. SOP 50 10 8 still requires a **10% equity injection from the buyer’s own unborrowed funds** on change-of-ownership, measured against **total project cost** (price plus allowable closing costs), and lenders often want more on goodwill-heavy deals ([SBA 7(a) for search/ETA acquisitions](https://acquisitionstars.com/blog/eta-sba-loan-structure)). On the memo’s own BizBuySell median (~$349k), that is **~$35–45k+** that cannot be a HELOC, cannot be “I’ll pay Scorta and recoup later,” and sits next to counsel and QoE.

The competitive wallet is not empty. [Acquisition Lab now charges $12,500](https://acquisitionlab.com/membership/pricing) for lifetime access, daily advisor Q&A, and deal reviews — and **explicitly tells members many lenders will reimburse the invoice at closing and roll it into the loan**. Walker Deibel’s own fee guide puts true buy-side advisors at **~$5,000/month retainers plus success fee**, aimed at PE off-market work, which is why he built Lab instead ([Who Pays What?](https://buythenbuild.com/who-pays-what/); [Deibel on fee structure](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/walkerdeibel_buying-a-small-business-the-fee-structure-activity-7407812081401614336-7IqD)).

So the memo’s price is:

- **Higher friction than Lab** (pay now, cannot wait for close, because you chose non-contingent to dodge Form 159).
- **Lower status than a real buy-side shop** (those charge $5k/mo and still don’t win first-time Main Street).
- **Due at LOI**, when earnest money, legal retainers, and QoE deposits all hit at once.

Buyers who can pay this without threatening injection are not the SBA-first corporate refugees the GTM names. Buyers who cannot pay it are the actual TAM. You are pricing against the people you claim to serve.

**Not neutralized.** “Priced as a fraction of accelerators” is a comparison to a product that is reimbursable at close and includes community. Your SKU is neither.

### 3. Regulatory surface

**Fatal if you do what the product description says. Not a week-3 checkbox.**

**Licensing.** The trigger is not “we called it advocacy.” It is negotiating a business sale for compensation, especially where a lease or “business opportunity” is involved. California treats business-opportunity negotiation as brokerage. Roughly 17 states route this through real estate commissions; lists disagree at the margin (Michigan/Illinois/Washington) which is itself an operational risk ([Business Brokerage Press state licensing](https://businessbrokeragepress.com/industry-resources/state-licensing/); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/do-business-brokers-need-a-license-state-by-state-rules/)). Main Street **is** a lease. HVAC, restaurants, landscaping — the Galindo anecdote — are premises businesses. “Design the service to stay clear of the lease” either (a) makes the service useless on the deals you cite, or (b) is a labeling trick that will not survive a commission complaint. Unlicensed fees can be unenforceable. National Searchfunder GTM into CA/FL/GA without a broker of record is not a launch plan.

**Form 159 / two-master.** This is the load-bearing legal error.

- Compensation to an Agent “**cannot be contingent on loan approval**” and must bear a “necessary and reasonable relationship to services actually performed” ([SBA Form 159 7(a) instructions](https://www.sba7a.loans/downloads/SBA-form-159-official.pdf); [13 CFR §103.5](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title13-vol1/pdf/CFR-2025-title13-vol1-part103.pdf)).
- **Agent includes** packager, referral agent, broker, accountant, attorney, consultant — anyone paid in connection with the loan.
- **§103.4(g)** bars being paid by applicant **and** lender on the same loan, with a **narrow** exception: Agent is a **Packager** paid by the applicant **and** a **Referral Agent** paid by the lender, with cross-disclosure. That exception is not “buyer’s advocate + referral agent.”
- CAFS Form 159 operations text is even cruder: the fee is paid by applicant **or** lender, **not both**.
- Combined Agent fees to the applicant are **capped** (order of magnitude: 3.5%/$10k on loans ≤$500k; 2%/$15k up to $1M; 1.5%/$30k above — [2020 SBA IFR on Agent fees](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2020-02128.pdf?1581083115=)). Your $5–10k deal-drive plus retainer plus anyone else’s packaging sits inside that cap.

The memo’s move — “a success fee contingent on **deal close** is not contingent on **loan approval**” — is the kind of distinction that dies in a lender’s credit committee, not in a blog post. Worse, the SKU includes “**lender packaging coordination**.” That is packaging. If you itemize it as packaging, the employee-interview / anti-cross-collateral work is outside the 159 description. If you itemize it as advocacy, you are an Agent on the loan **and** you cannot also take the lender point.

**Practical result:** the hybrid GTM is illegal or self-cannibalizing. Every advocacy dollar on a file blocks the “recurring engine” on that file. “Default to one per deal” means the $48k model and the lender thesis cannot both be true for the same customer.

**Dual agency.** Keep-sell-side-book-as-deal-flow while acting buy-side is how first-time buyers decide you are dumping inventory. Disclosure does not fix the inference.

**Citizenship.** Effective March 1, 2026, **100% of direct and indirect owners** of a 7(a)/504 applicant must be U.S. citizens or nationals with principal residence in the U.S.; LPRs are out ([SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441](https://www.sba.gov/document/policy-notice-5000-876441-update-sop-50-10-8-citizenship-residency-requirements-recission-procedural-notice-5000-872050)). That shrinks the buyer pool the memo is selling into and **increases** the value of an eligibility screen sold to someone else (franchisors, packagers). It does not increase willingness of a disqualified or barely qualified searcher to pay you $10k.

**Not neutralized.** “Confirm with Starfield & Smith in week one” is not a control. It is a hope that counsel will bless a structure the regulation already describes.

### 4. Timing and the $30k non-contingent floor

**Fatal.**

Self-funded search to close is typically **12–36 months**; LOI to close on SBA is **60–120 days** ([Acquisition Stars](https://acquisitionstars.com/blog/search-fund-eta-legal-guide)). Deal-drive is priced at LOI. **3–4 deal-drive fees per month by week 8** requires a standing book of buyers already in exclusivity. You do not have that book. People already at LOI have counsel. People in month two of Searchfunder browsing do not have an LOI.

Lender referral onboarding is weeks to months; the memo admits this and still uses it as co-anchor. Decline-remediation requires a lender to share declined applications (NPI, GLBA, vendor DDQ). That is not week 8.

The collected-floor arithmetic:

| SKU | Plan wk 11 | What has to be true | Base case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screens | 8 × $1k = $8k | 8 paid screens/month from a new buy-side motion | 1–3 if the warm list is real |
| Deal-drive | 3–4 × $7.5k = $25k | 3–4 LOIs/month in week 8 | ~0 |
| Retainers | 20 × $750 = $15k | 20 concurrent paying searchers by week 10 | 0–2 |
| Lender points | parked as backlog | ≥1 funded 7(a) in 9–13 weeks | 0 collected |

**Base case collected: $3–8k.** The $30k floor is not missed by 20%. It is missed because two of three SKUs do not exist in week 11.

Note the company’s own history: seller diagnostics already produced a **$24k month (May 2026)** with nine owners. The buy-side plan asks you to **leave a demonstrated payer** for an undemonstrated one.

- **Actual buy-side (CT Acquisitions, Quiet Light, etc.):** success-fee shops; CT’s published posture is **no retainer, no exclusivity** — the opposite of your SKU — and they hunt LMM/PE add-ons, not $349k landscaping.

- **Packagers / referral agents:** already sit on Form 159. They will not welcome a new dual-compensated “advocate” in the file.

The memo says buy-side is “fragmented and un-productized.” Correct translation: **the representation product failed in this band**, so the market productized education (Lab) and origination rails (LoanBud) instead. You are proposing to relaunch the failed product with better copy.

### 7. Adverse selection and deal quality

**High-probability.**

Listing brokers tolerate a buyer’s advocate when the listing is dying. Those are the deals where advocacy cannot save DSCR, owner-dependency, or a rotten lease. Buyers who pay retainers before they have a target are, in this market, often the ones who cannot get to LOI without hand-holding — i.e. the unfinanceable. Screens that honestly say “this fails SOP” produce refund pressure. Screens that stretch to keep the monthly create Galindo-class failures with your name on the invoice.

If you also have a sell-side book, the adverse selection is internal: you will be tempted to walk retained buyers toward inventory you need to move.

**Not neutralized.** Flagging co-broke adverse selection and then selling advocacy into the same listings is not a control.

### 9. Unit economics and the two-person constraint

**Fatal.**

Hours to deliver the **plan**, not the company:

- 4 deal-drives × ~40h = 160h (employee access fights, WIP, structure, packaging)
- 20 retainers × ~2h = 40h
- 8 screens × ~4h = 32h  
**≈ 232h/month of delivery** before selling, 17-state counsel, lender BD, and building agents.

Two founders have maybe **~80h/month** of delivery if they are also the product org. The only way the math closes is if “advocacy” is a model-written memo plus a Zoom. That is the **report the memo already killed**. You cannot charge representation prices for report delivery and also claim the Galindo failure modes require a fighter.

Conversion math is the same fantasy in reverse. 20 retainers by week 10, at even a heroic 25% screen-to-retainer and 20% conversation-to-screen, implies hundreds of qualified conversations. Acquisition Lab is a brand with a bestselling book. You are two people with a seller-side diagnostic and no buy-side reputation.

### 10. Hidden conflicts and first-time-buyer trust

**High-probability.**

First-time buyers’ actual trusted stack: their CPA, their attorney, Lab Slack, a Searchfunder thread. A two-founder brokerage that (a) has or had a sell-side book, (b) wants to route the 7(a) to a partner lender, and (c) promises “we never double-dip” is indistinguishable from every broker who ever double-dipped. They cannot audit you. [Practitioners already write about brokers collecting listing commission and lender referral on the same SBA deal](https://www.ehowardlaw.com/post/how-business-brokers-double-dip-on-sba-deals-without-telling-you-legal-but-unethical). Your policy memo is not a trust product.

If the buyer ever sees Form 159 with your name in two places, the relationship ends and you have a regulatory problem. If they never see it, you have an ethics problem. There is no third state.

### 11. Advocate vs report, under deal pressure

**Fatal as a differentiator.**

The Galindo story is not a product spec. It is a map of **who has power**:

| Failure mode | Who can actually stop it | What Scorta can do at $5–10k |
|---|---|---|
| No pre-close employee conversations | Seller and listing broker, who routinely forbid it | Insist, and watch the listing broker kill the deal to protect the seller |
| WIP / undisclosed liabilities | Buyer’s counsel + QoE | A checklist. Maybe a good one. Not a finding that survives indemnity negotiation |
| Cross-collateralization on the add-on | The lender’s credit box / SOP collateral rules | “Refuse” and lose the loan, or accept and fail the brand promise |

Add-on 7(a)s that put two leveraged businesses under one guarantor are how that product works. An outsider “refusing” the bank is theater. The reason these failure modes persist is not that nobody thought of them. It is that the parties who can force them (seller, broker, lender) are not paid by the first-time buyer’s $7.5k.

**Romanticism:** “a report does not fight.” Correct. **Also correct:** you will not fight either, because fighting without leverage is how you lose the only deal that might have paid you, and at this price you cannot afford the hours.

### B. Alternative paths

#### Path 1 — Change-of-ownership underwriting OS (primary)

**Thesis:** The scarce object is not an advocate. It is a 7(a) change-of-ownership **file** that survives SOP 50 10 8, transcripts vs recast, equity-injection source tests, affiliation, and citizenship — sold to the people whose P&L moves when that file funds.

**Who pays and why:** Independent packagers, non-bank 7(a) shops, high-velocity LOs. They already eat unbillable redo hours when a CoO file dies in underwriting. A funded loan is origination income plus, for many, gain-on-sale. They are not conserving a down payment. They buy weekly.

**Product:** Agent-native CoO workbench: recast-to-tax variance, SOP credit-box, 8821/4506-C packet hygiene, injection-source test (gift vs borrowed vs ROBS), affiliation + post-March-2026 citizenship screen, 159-clean fee schedule. **Seat or per-file, payable on delivery, never on approval.** If counsel says you are an Agent, you are a packager inside the cap and you **never** take lender-side compensation on that loan.

**Why prior roadblocks collapse:** No co-broke. No buyer injection fight. No 17-state brokerage if you are a vendor/tool to a licensed packager/lender (confirm in writing; if false, stay inside packaging and the cap). No buyer churn. Two-master avoided by **one payer per file**. Lender procurement lag avoided by selling to **packagers first**, not Live Oak vendor management.

**11-week shape:** Week 1–2: 10 packager/LO calls, 3 paid historical-file redos. Week 3–6: FDE on stalled/declined CoO files at $1.5–5k/file. Week 7–11: 2–3 shops on residual ($2–4k/mo or file quota). **Collected $12–25k from this motion alone; $30k if you do not shut off seller diagnostics.** Key kill tests: see Part 4.

**Remaining risks:** LoanBud already owns “badge + apply to 50 lenders.” You only win if the unpaid gap is **recast-vs-tax / injection / affiliation**, not “is this listing SBA-flavored.” Form 159 if your SOW is actually packaging. FDE time vs two-person constraint — this still has delivery load, but the **payer can pay for it** and the work matches the stack you already built (Recast, Boardroom-as-underwriter, Lender Ops).

#### Path 2 — Franchise SBA + citizenship gate (orthogonal)

**Thesis:** On March 1, 2026 the SBA made LPR ownership a hard ineligibility. Franchisors and franchise-development shops are still running pipelines that assume green-card operators can get 7(a). Sell a **candidate-level SBA/citizenship/credit-box kill-switch** into a channel that already budgets CAC (franchise brokers are paid 40–50% of the franchise fee by the **franchisor** — the memo’s own “structural payer” analogy, applied where it actually holds).

**Who pays:** Franchisor VP of development / preferred-lender coordinators / high-volume franchise brokers. They pay to stop burning FDD cycles and broker comp on candidates who cannot close.

**Product:** 48-hour candidate screen: citizenship/residency, injection source, personal credit box, brand-level SBA Franchise Directory issues, then a deeper financeability pass. API or per-candidate ($300–800) plus a $2–5k/mo seat.

**Why prior roadblocks collapse:** Not M&A representation. Not a first-time buyer writing a check against a down payment. Not co-broke. Repeat recruitment, not one close. Regulatory pull is **current**, not theoretical.

**11-week shape:** 1–2 brand or broker-network pilots. $8–20k if a live pipeline is put through the screen. Kill if you cannot get a development lead on a call by week 2 — this is a different Rolodex than Searchfunder.

**Remaining risks:** “SBA preferred lender list” already exists as a slide in every FDD process. You must attach to **disqualification cost** (wasted broker comp, 90-day dead deals post-citizenship rule), not “we also score financeability.” Distribution into franchise orgs is a new GTM.

---

#### Path 3 — Seller diagnostic as cash bridge, CPA as the repeat surface

**Thesis:** Do not invent a buy-side GTM to hit $30k. You have already collected from owners. The honest 11-week cash engine is the **seller diagnostic**; the durable customer is the **CPA book**, not the seller and not the first-time buyer.

**Who pays:** Owners (one-time, proven) and tax/advisory firms (repeat, per-seat, on their existing clients). Owners pay because a bankability miss is a six-figure price cut on the memo’s own restaurant example. CPAs pay because exit conversations are already their relationship and they will not become brokers.

**Product:** Unchanged diagnostic / Scorta Score as a **cash and data channel**. CPA SKU is the actual residual. Brokerage success fee stays off the 11-week floor.

**Why prior roadblocks collapse:** Avoids buyer cash, co-broke, two-master (if you do not also refer the buyer’s loan), and the LOI timing fantasy. Licensing is still gray if the diagnostic shades into listing, so **keep it a diagnostic** in the sprint.

**11-week shape:** This is the only path with a historical $20k+ month. Do not pause it to go hunting searchers.

**Remaining risks:** Still not a venture-scale company by itself. Seller is one-time. If you turn the diagnostic into de facto brokerage in license states, you reimport P5. YC will not fund “we do more GAP reports” unless it is explicitly the data-acquisition front end for Path 1.

---

#### Path 4 — Acquisition transcript / recast-variance layer (picks and shovels)

**Thesis:** Mortgage vendors already productized 4506-C rejection. **Change-of-ownership** needs something they do not sell: a multi-entity transcript stack (seller 1120S/1065 + owner 1040/K-1 + buyer 1040) reconciled to the **recast the lender will actually underwrite**. The product is the variance report, not IVES access as such.

**Who pays:** Same packagers/LOs as Path 1, per file. This can be a **module of Path 1** rather than a separate company — and should be, unless a packager will pay for it standalone in week 3.

**Why prior roadblocks collapse:** Orthogonal to advocacy. Not buyer-paid. Not brokerage. Repeat per loan.

**11-week shape:** Only as an attach to Path 1 paid redos. Standalone, you lose to Tax Guard on speed-to-transcript and you still need IVES/8821 partnership.

**Remaining risks:** [Tax Guard](https://tax-guard.com/blog/what-do-sba-lenders-need-to-know-about-the-irs-form-8821/) and [Certified Credit](https://www.certifiedcredit.com/the-4506-c-problem-delays-rejections-and-how-to-protect-your-pipeline/) already own the “we’ll get the transcripts” job. Your only unique surface is **recast-vs-tax for CoO**, which is Path 1’s core loop. Do not build a second GTM for a module.

| 2 | SOW can stay off Form 159 as vendor/FDE | Written memo from SBA/lending counsel on the exact scope | If you are an Agent: packaging only, inside the cap, **no** lender-side fee on that loan. If counsel will not put it in writing, **stop selling**. | Day 10 |
| 3 | Output changes underwriting, not the PDF | On paid redos, packager/lender records a disposition change (resubmit / approve / documented real decline) | **Kill** if 0/5 files change disposition. Vanity recasts do not count. | Day 35 |
| 4 | $30k floor without buyer retainers | Trailing-30-day **collected** from packagers + existing seller diagnostics only | **Kill as batch engine** if <$20k by week 8. No credit for unpaid LOIs or promised points. | Week 8 |
| 5 | Repeat customer exists | Seat or monthly file quota signed | **Kill** if 0 shops convert project → residual by week 11. One heroic file is not a company. | Week 11 |
| 6 | LoanBud does not already satisfy the buyer | Every packager call: what they use today; what is still Excel | **Kill** if the unpaid gap is “send the buyer to LoanBud.” **Proceed** only if the gap is recast-vs-tax / injection source / affiliation. | Week 2 |
| 7 | Franchise gate (secondary) | 2 development orgs run the screen on live candidates | **Kill** if 0 pay ≥$2k for a 30-day pilot. **Proceed** if ≥1 paid pilot and ≥20 candidates run. | Week 5 |
| 8 | $299 buyer screen (optional) | Stripe checkout, no sales call required | **Kill** if <25 paid in 21 days. **Forbidden:** upsell into retainers or deal-drive during the probe. | Day 21 |

**Forbidden rescue moves:** extending because “lenders are slow”; counting a signed referral agreement as revenue; relabeling a model memo as advocacy to hit $30k; taking buyer fees and lender fees on the same loan because “we disclosed it” when you are not inside the packager/referral exception; pausing seller diagnostics to go find searchers.

---

If Path 1 dies on tests 1, 3, or 6, the company does not pivot back to advocacy. It has learned that even the **file** is not scarce enough to bill. At that point the honest options are Path 2 (different payer, same engine) or Path 3 (services cash, no infrastructure story). Advocacy is not on that list.
