What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.

Scorta is an AI-native business brokerage that sells the full service (not software) for owner-operated
Main Street businesses ($500K–$5M revenue). We do everything a traditional broker does — valuation,
financial packaging, buyer materials, buyer qualification, lender coordination, and diligence but AI does
the repeatable work, so we can profitably serve the bottom 80% of the market that human brokers
ignore. Owners pay us a flat fee instead of a 10% commission. Our first product, ExitIQ, is the AI
assessment that becomes the intake step for every engagement: it produces a valuation range, sale￾readiness plan, and lender-readiness view. Long term, Scorta is how every Main Street business in
America gets sold.

Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know
people need what you're making?

Suyash ran a micro-PE fund for 3 years acquiring Main Street businesses — closed 2 deals and
evaluated hundreds more. He watched deal after deal collapse for the same reasons: brokers were
unresponsive, sellers were unprepared, financials were a mess, and SBA financing fell through because
no one had set the deal up to be financeable. The bottleneck wasn't buyer demand — buyers were
everywhere. The bottleneck was that no one was getting these businesses ready to sell. Brokers don't fix
it because their economics don't work on sub-$2M deals; they list and pray. Puneet and Suyash are both
AI engineers (Travelers and Atlassian, both building AI tooling), and have been building together for 10
years — we previously built and sold a marketing company as cofounders at 18. We started Scorta
because Suyash needed it to exist as a buyer, and three sellers paid us within weeks of us offering to do
the work for them.

Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?

What we understand that they don't: the bottleneck in Main Street M&A isn't matching, listing, or running
an auction. It's that most of these businesses can't pass an SBA lender's underwriting in their current
state. Brokers won't do that prep work because human time doesn't pencil on small deals. AI does. So
we own the prep layer everyone else assumes someone else will handle, and that earns us the right to
run the transaction too. Three categories of competition. Traditional brokers (Transworld, Sunbelt, local
independents) handle Main Street deals at 10–12% commission but only take listings they can sell with
minimal prep. They run the process through email and PDFs and are conflict-averse on telling owners
hard truths about valuation. Marketplaces (BizBuySell, Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers, Baton,
Quiet Light) are listing sites. They don't prepare the seller, qualify the buyer, or manage the transaction.
OffDeal is the closest analog — AI-native investment bank for SMBs. They run sell-side auctions for
$2M–$10M+ businesses that are already clean enough for a process. We start one tier below: $500K–
$2M Main Street businesses that aren't sellable yet.

How do or will you make money? How much could you make?

Flat fees per transaction. Today: $3K–$5K for prep-only engagements. Moving to $15K–$30K flat for full￾service "broker replacement" engagements as automation matures. A 10% commission on a $1M sale is
$100K+ — we're materially cheaper for the seller and structurally more profitable for us because AI does
the labor. Bottom-up TAM: ~10,000 Main Street businesses sell through brokers in the US each year at
~$1M average sale price. At 10% commission, that's ~$1B/year in broker fees — the directly
addressable market we're displacing. The bigger opportunity is the businesses that don't sell. There are
~2.9M owner-operated Main Street businesses in the US, with ~10,000 baby boomers retiring per day.
Most owners who want to sell can't, because their business isn't sellable. Making them sellable expands
the transactable supply 5–10x. At a $20K blended average revenue per transaction, capturing 5% of the
existing brokered market is $10M ARR. Capturing meaningful share of a 5x-larger market we help create
is a clear path to $500M+ ARR. Future revenue lines: SBA lender referral fees, buyer-side success fees,
transaction infrastructure (escrow, closing) take rates, and a buyer-side product for acquirers like the
micro-PE funds Suyash came from.
