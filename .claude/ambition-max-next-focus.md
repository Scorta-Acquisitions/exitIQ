## 2. Closest big idea

Stripe for small-business M&A, in the form you can actually touch this quarter: a **managed marketplace whose scarce asset is financeable supply**.

- Sellers stay in a permanently sellable state because you sit in the tools they already use.
- Buyers trust the package because you have already recast the books against a legal 7(a) stack.
- Brokers can take the overflow until your own buyer density is real.

### Seller rail (primary)

Agentic connect into QuickBooks, Gusto, Stripe, POS — not a new system of record. Recast. Owner-dependency and concentration flags. One-page verdict: **financeable as-is**, **financeable if restructured**, or **not on 7(a)**. Continuous “keep sellable” rescores (the Boardroom / Drata move from Summer GT, pulled forward as the acquisition product).

- **Price to the seller now:** free or near-free.
- **Take-rate later:** success fee if you run the process, wholesale split if a broker does, optional paid remediation if they want the grade to move.

### Buyer rail (secondary)

A free curated off-market list of SBA-financeable packages, plus the DealIQ fundability map so a searcher can see whether this deal clears their cash. That is how “we already have buyers” stops being a pitch and becomes true.

This is a **trust layer, not the Demo Day company**.

Optional $20 seat or Structure Pack is fine as a side SKU for people already on the list. Do not staff ten engineers against $299 MRR.

| Summer GT piece | What changes now | What stays |

| NJ/NY/CT + three verticals | Keep as the agent-loop constraint so volume is reproducible | Home services, restaurants, marketing agencies |

### Pete’s line, as an operating sentence

If by November you have a surprisingly large number of businesses whose books are connected, recast, and SBA-screened — and you can show that a human operator can run that loop at high margin — you have pulled off the magic trick. Closings can still be lumpy. Searcher MRR can be a footnote.
