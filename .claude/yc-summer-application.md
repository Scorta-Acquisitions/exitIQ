What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.
Scorta is an AI-native business brokerage that sells the full service (not software) for owner-operated Main Street businesses ($500K–$5M revenue).

We do everything a traditional broker does: valuation, financial packaging, buyer materials, buyer qualification, lender coordination, and diligence -> but two coordinated teams of AI agents do the repeatable work.
The first team turns an unstructured business into a sellable asset: recasting financials, mapping owner dependency, pre-qualifying with SBA lenders.
The second team builds the deal room, lists on marketplaces and runs coordinated outreach to capital-verified buyers in the right industry and size segment.

This lets us profitably serve the bottom 80% of the market that human brokers ignore.
Owners pay a $3-5K flat fee for the first team and a 5% success fee on close: half of what traditional brokers charge.

Our first product, ExitIQ, is the AI assessment that becomes the intake step for every engagement: it produces a valuation range, sale-readiness plan, and lender-readiness view.

Long term, Scorta is how every Main Street business in America gets sold.

Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you’re making?
Suyash ran a micro-PE fund for 2 years acquiring Main Street businesses (2 closed, hundreds evaluated). He watched deal after deal collapse for the same reasons: brokers were unresponsive, sellers were unprepared, financials were a mess, and SBA financing fell through because no one had set the deal up to be financeable. The bottleneck wasn't buyer demand, the buyers were everywhere. The bottleneck was that no one was getting these businesses ready to sell. Brokers don't fix it because their economics don't work on sub-$2M deals; they list and pray. We are both AI engineers (Travelers and Atlassian, both of us building production grade AI features), and have been building together for 10 years — we previously built and sold a marketing company as cofounders at 18. We started Scorta because Suyash needed it to exist as a buyer, and three sellers paid us within weeks of us offering to do the work for them.
Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don’t?
What we understand that they don't: the bottleneck in Main Street M&A isn't matching, listing, or running an auction. It's that most of these businesses can't pass an SBA lender's underwriting in their current state. Brokers won't do that prep work because human time doesn't pencil on small deals. AI does. So we own the prep layer everyone else assumes someone else will handle, and that earns us the right to run the transaction too.

Three categories of competition.

Traditional brokers (Transworld, Sunbelt, local independents) handle Main Street deals at 10–12% commission but only take listings they can sell with minimal prep. They run the process through email and PDFs and are conflict-averse on telling owners hard truths about valuation.

Marketplaces (BizBuySell, Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers, Baton, Quiet Light) are listing sites. They don't prepare the seller, qualify the buyer, or manage the transaction.
OffDeal is the closest analog — AI-native investment bank for SMBs. They run sell-side auctions for $2M–$10M+ businesses that are already clean enough for a process. We start one tier below: $500K–$2M Main Street businesses that aren't sellable yet.
How do or will you make money? How much could you make?
Two-part pricing per transaction: $3K–$5K flat for the prep work, then a 5% success fee when the business sells. Total revenue on a $1M sale: ~$55K. A traditional broker takes 10% commission ($100K+) and does little prep work, we're half the price and our prep work delivered an expected 20% valuation lift for our first two brokerage clients.

Structurally profitable because AI does the labor: variable cost per engagement today is ~$400 in AI inference and infrastructure, and one operator at Scorta can comfortably run many times more deals that a traditional brokershop.

US market: 150,000 sub-$2M Main Street transactions per year nationally. At ~$55K average revenue per closed deal, the broker-replacement opportunity is ~$10B/year. Layer on lender subscriptions, CPA per-seat licenses, and certification licensing once the Scorta Score becomes the industry standard — multiples of that.
