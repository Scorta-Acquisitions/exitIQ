## Executive verdict

**Revised probability of a strong, fundable Demo Day under this plan as written: ~15–20%.**

The Continuity Evidence object is still the highest-expected-value *wedge* for the Stripe-for-Main-Street-M&A thesis. The 11-week *operating plan* is not. It is a conjunctive science fair that zeros a company that already has paid prep, closed deals, and a warm CPA motion, then asks two founders to sign six randomized TX/TN partners in five days, convert 10% of untriggered close-cohort invitations at $1,500, complete owner-dependent repairs in peak HVAC season, and call twenty VROs plus two non-close “activations” a transfer rail.

That is how you miss Demo Day with a true insight.

**Is Continuity Evidence + professional-first still the right path?** Yes, as the *object* and the *payer architecture*. No, as this sprint protocol. Highest EV for this team and this thesis is: keep the verified record, sell a finite triggered job through the warm professional channel you already have, do not reset geography or customers to zero, and treat VRO purity as a quality bar rather than a week-2 company-stop.

**The single sentence a partner would say in office hours:** you already proved owners pay $3–5k for sale-prep and that a CPA can send HVAC work. This plan tests a colder, cheaper, slower motion in new states, then writes a kill switch that fires before the experiment can teach you anything. Pete’s bar was a surprisingly large number of businesses in a financeable state at high margin. Twenty $1,500 VROs and $0 transaction revenue is a regression from the book you already have.

## Steelman (strongest version, before the attack)

The market has already priced owner-paid always-on exit planning at approximately zero. [SMB.co prices](https://smb.co/pricing) the owner at $0/month plus 1% at close and the adviser at $299/month. [Baton](https://www.baton.com/pricing) charges when the owner enters a sale process ($1,000/month credited at close, 6% of the first $5M, 2% above). OffDeal leads with $0 up front. Maus / Value Builder sell the recurring system to advisers, not a finite owner sprint. ideas42’s 2025 interviews (cited in the plan) show why: 63% of owners without a plan said it was too early, 80% thought owners should wait until they felt ready, 43% said they were too busy.

So the honest product is not another score, CIM factory, or AI broker. It is the missing object underneath all of them: a permissioned, source-proven record of whether the business can run without the owner, which earnings are supported, what blocks transfer, and which repairs survived professional review. Sell that as a finite job at a *present* failure (payroll authority, earnings support, one repair). Sell the recurring workspace to the CPA/CAS/broker who already has the client and the budget. Activate a competitive process only for businesses that earned readiness. That is the only path from “prep shop” to “verified operating and financing layer” that does not require pretending owners will subscribe, or that you can out-bank OffDeal.

The VRO definition (paid + connected sources + reviewed + verified action + minutes tracked) is the right anti-vanity metric. The four-engineer product cap in the first fortnight is the right anti-overbuild rule. The CPA rights split (client, data, judgment, activation) is the right way to turn the incumbent from an enemy into a channel. Competitive reconciliation is directionally correct: do not claim novelty for assessments, dashboards, or data rooms.

If this team were starting from a true zero with eleven weeks and no existing book, this would be a serious seed thesis. They are not starting from zero. The sprint protocol pretends they are.

## What “strong Demo Day” would have to look like

Under *this* plan’s own success case: 20 VROs, 60 paid (20 owner-paid), 6 repeat partners, a matched-file reviewer study at ≥30% less screen time, two licensed non-exclusive activations, **$0 transaction revenue**.

That is a **seed-fluent design-partner story**, not a transfer-rail story. Deep research on Demo Day / seed narrative puts 60 paid sprints at $1,500–$7,500 in the $90k–$450k collected band — enough to prove a real job, not enough to prove a network. Six first-cycle partners is a hand-picked signal, not multi-cycle channel PMF (that bar is closer to 18–24 partner-cycles). Two activations that are not closes do not substitute for GMV.

Meanwhile the company’s own fall application already reports **$173k collected, two closes ($1.3M and $900k → $110k success fees), one $1.7M under contract, 18 paying customers, three DealIQ pilots**. If the sprint “succeeds” by the plan’s lights and the existing book is treated as out of scope, Demo Day numbers get *worse*. If the existing book is quietly kept, the experiment is not the experiment the gates describe.

A partner-fundable Demo Day for *this* thesis in eleven weeks is: a surprisingly large number of connected, recast, financeable-state packages (Pete, 14 Aug); two to four professionals who send a second cohort without founder substitution; reviewer evidence that the file reduced rework; **and** the transaction proof you already have, left on the scoreboard. Twenty perfect VROs without that combination will be read as a high-touch continuity consultancy.

## Ranked sprint-breaking risks

Highest severity first. Likelihood is inside this 11-week window if the plan is followed as written.

### 1. Week-1 partner machine does not exist

**Failure mode.** Six CPA/P&C firms, each with a preregistered denominator of ~50 eligible HVAC/plumbing/electrical owners, contracted in five business days to send their own copy, run two every-nth batches from a real August close/renewal cohort, and refuse hand-picking. Directory math does not support this. CPA.com’s 2024 CAS survey supports CAS growth (median 17%), not “50 eligible trade clients per firm.” A mid-size CAS book of ~69 clients, if 5–12% are HVAC/plumbing/electrical, has **3–8 trade clients**, not 50. Six firms with 50 eligible trade clients each are specialist trade shops. Those are not signed from QuickBooks ProAdvisor / TXCPA / IIAT cold outreach in week 1, with owner-pull consent, no contingent referral pay, and a randomized protocol CPAs generally reject because it exposes messy files and removes reputational curation.

**Likelihood:** high (~70–80% that you do not have six contracted, two-batch, non-hand-picking partners by 21 Aug).

**Mitigation inside the batch:** yes, but it violates the plan. Use the CPA and consultant reseller you already have; drop the 50-eligible-client filter and the every-nth purity for the first fortnight; accept two warm partners instead of six cold ones. There is no realistic mitigation that keeps the written week-1 spec.

This is the first conjunctive kill. If partners fail, 120 invitations fail, 12 paid fails, Aug 28 fires, and the remaining nine weeks are spent arguing about whether to “stop scaling.”

### 2. Aug 28 conversion math is 2–4× optimistic on an untriggered cohort

**Failure mode.** 12 paid of 120 invitations (10%) and 6 owner-paid (5%) from partner-owned close/renewal copy, not from “owner said they might sell.” Deep-research first-principles: 120 partner invitations → ~20–35% engaged (24–42) → 4–10% of engaged pay for unsolicited advisory → **~1–3 paid**, not 12. A 10%/5% hit rate is plausible only if the partner pre-filters to owners already in a trigger (succession mention, “not ready but fixable,” lender request, 6–24 month horizon). The plan forbids that filter.

“Present failure” (payroll, bank, dispatch) is the right *script* and will beat “18–36 months of exit planning.” It does not beat August HVAC peak season, owner-pull permission, and $1,500 for a job the owner did not ask for. The classic “I’ll get to it later” objection is not eliminated by copy; it is eliminated by a trigger. ideas42 is evidence *against* untriggered owner payment. The plan cites it, then designs an untriggered funnel.

Your own book contradicts the $1,500 untriggered test: HVAC owners already paid **$5k** when a CPA referred them into sale-prep. You are proposing to test a colder cohort at a lower price in new states, then treat miss as thesis failure.

**Likelihood:** high on the written 12/6 bar (~65–75% miss if invitations are truly every-nth); medium if partners quietly hand-pick.

**Mitigation:** sell only to triggered owners at the $3–5k you already cleared; count sponsor-paid as a separate line, not a consolation prize; do not make 6 owner-paid a week-2 kill. The authorized 7-day sponsor retest does not save you if the invitation engine never starts.

### 3. Verified repair cannot be scheduled into week 2

**Failure mode.** A VRO requires a verified action, not a PDF. The plan’s examples (second payroll approver + live payroll drill; move customer ownership to a service manager; one-day owner absence) are owner-latency jobs. Biweekly payroll, peak season, and “I can’t be off the truck in August” mean many paid, connected owners will not complete a repair by 28 Aug. You then have paid customers who are not VROs, and the conjunctive gate treats that as stop.

Delivery hours compound this. First-time source-connected evidence + one repair + external review is realistically **6.0–8.5 hours** of *your* time, plus days of *their* time. The plan’s <4h fully loaded by case 6, at $100/hour, is a spreadsheet. CAS special-project loaded rates sit closer to $200–350/hour. At 7h × $100 the pilot GM looks fine; at 8h × $250 plus founder sales minutes plus CAC, the $1,500 SKU is negative and the “software-like margin” claim is theater.

**Likelihood:** high that you do not have 6 verified repairs by 28 Aug even if 12 people pay (~60–70%).

**Mitigation:** split “paid + connected + reviewed” from “verified repair.” Require the repair by 18 Sep, not 28 Aug. Pick repairs that can complete inside a close cycle (document a second signer, not run a full absence week). Do not call a completed checklist a repair.

### 4. Zeroing the existing book is a Demo Day own-goal

**Failure mode.** Starting position in the plan: “no customers, no distribution.” Your documents say otherwise: $173k, two closes, a $1.7M under-contract file, 15 sell-side prep customers, three paid DealIQ pilots, one CPA referral that produced three customers, a consultant reseller. Office hours (14 Aug) already showed sell-side intake is hard and the only owner hook that works is “we already have buyers.” Ambition-max (15 Aug) correctly put 70% of founder time on seller volume and free/near-free prep. This plan (17 Aug) is the fourth 11-week company in two weeks. A partner will not underwrite a team that resets the scoreboard every Sunday.

If you follow the plan, you spend the batch acquiring 120 cold invitations in TX/TN instead of converting the NJ/NYC book and the $1.7M file. Transaction revenue is **defined as $0 in the success case**. That is not discipline. That is leaving the only Demo Day number investors already understand off the slide.

**Likelihood:** certain if the plan is followed; the damage is narrative, not hypothetical.

**Mitigation:** keep brokerage runoff and DealIQ as a second line. Do not move the Continuity Evidence test to TX/TN until one warm channel repeats. Do not write “zero customers” into the operating system.

### 5. Conjunctive Aug 28 gate kills a live idea on a distribution miss

**Failure mode.** Continue only if **all** of: 6 partners, 120 delivered eligible invitations, 12 paid including 6 owner-paid, 4 independent batch-twos, 8 dual-source connections, 6 reviews <4h, 6 verified actions, 5/10 blinded reviewer changes. Independent 50% events would yield ~0.4% joint probability. Even correlated, the joint bar is built to fail. The first miss (partners) makes every later number undefined, so you never get a clean read on WTP, delivery, or reviewer value — the three things that actually matter.

**Likelihood:** very high that the written conjunctive test fails (~85%+). Medium that founders rewrite the gate under pressure, which is how “kill gates that don’t kill” come back.

**Mitigation:** make Aug 28 diagnostic, not a company-stop. Sequence: (1) can we get invitations out, (2) do triggered owners pay and connect, (3) can delivery be timed, (4) does a reviewer change behavior. Kill a *layer* when that layer fails. Do not kill the thesis because week-1 BD missed.

## Ranked serious-but-not-fatal concerns

### 1. Permission and eligibility density

Owner-pull consent is correct and slow. Dual-source (QuickBooks + bank/payroll/CRM/field service) will miss a large share of paid owners. First-principles filter stack on a true 50-trade book: license ~90–94%, revenue band + real books ~70–80%, independent source ~60–75% of remainder → **~28–40% still eligible** before WTP. The plan’s 8/12 connected (67%) is the optimistic tail. This is a Sep problem if week 1 somehow works; it is fatal only if you treat 8/12 as a week-2 kill.

### 2. Partner fatigue, substitution, and competitive fear

Even a signed CPA will founder-substitute, hand-pick, or stall on batch two. The economic pitch (“add a fixed-fee advisory offer, keep the client”) is the right pitch. The fear (“you are a broker in a hoodie”) is rational given your public brokerage history and 5% success-fee agreements. One partner who hears “activation” in week 3 poisons the rest. Manageable if activation rights are in the contract on day one and founders do not freelance brokerage onto partner files.

### 3. Reviewer study is the right asset and the wrong week-2 instrument

A blinded matched-file test with unaffiliated lenders is the single best Demo Day substitute for missing GMV. You will not recruit 10 honest blinded reviewers and complete 6 reviewed files by 28 Aug. Move the 5/10 “material change” test to Sep 18 / Oct 16 where the plan already puts the 10-file comparison. Early “reviewer change” will be friends, and partners will smell it.

### 4. $1,500 is a WTP instrument, not the SKU

After you already sold HVAC at $5k, $1,500 teaches the wrong ceiling and attracts the wrong owners (curious, not triggered). Use $1,500 only as a discount against a $3–5k triggered sprint, or as a partner-sponsored line. The later $3k/$5k/$7.5k A/B is the real price test; it cannot run if the cheap SKU is the only thing that clears Aug 28.

### 5. Delivery compression to <3h after case 20 vs 12 VROs by 18 Sep

The Sep 18 gate wants median <3h after case 20 **and** 12 VROs. You cannot have case 20 if VRO count is 12 unless “case” ≠ VRO. The credible mature floor with a reviewer still in the loop is ~3.0–3.75h. Below that you lose the “externally reviewed” claim. Software-like 80% GM at 1.5h is a 2027 number. Honest 55–65% GM on a $5k triggered sprint is fundable. Fake 3h at $1,500 is not.

### 6. Two “safe activations” by 30 Oct from *new* Continuity owners

New owners who paid for a 10-day continuity sprint will not also sign a licensed mandate in eleven weeks. If you need two activations for the narrative, they come from the existing pipeline, and you must say so. Counting them as Continuity Evidence conversions is fraud on yourselves.

### 7. Strategy whiplash as an investor-diligence item

14 Aug: buy-side for Demo Day MRR. Pete: don’t pivot for that. 15 Aug: free prep, 50+ businesses, DealIQ is not the company. 17 Aug: paid Continuity Evidence, TX/TN, 10 engineers, conjunctive stop. A partner who sees this sequence will discount every gate in the new plan. Fix by picking one hill-climb and not producing a fifth company next Sunday.

## First-principles math (plan vs adverse)

**Aug 28 funnel**

| Step | Plan | Adverse (untriggered, partner-copy) | Triggered (CPA-flagged, your historical analog) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Invitations delivered | 120 | 40–80 (partner slip, eligibility) | 30–40 from 2 warm books |
| Engaged | implied ~120 | 24–42 of 120; fewer if invites slip | 15–25 |
| Paid | 12 (10%) | 1–3 | 6–12 at $3–5k |
| Owner-paid | 6 | 0–2 | 4–8 |

**Partner density.** 6 × 50 eligible trade clients = 300 HVAC/plumbing/electrical names in-band in two states in week 1. A generalist CAS firm contributes ~3–8. You need specialist shops or you need to drop the 50-client gate.

**Unit economics.** Plan’s first ten: $1,500 price, 7h, $100/h, $100 tools, $1,000 CAC → negative contribution after CAC. That honesty is good. Replacing $100/h with $200–250 loaded and adding founder BD minutes makes the $1,500 SKU a learning product only. A $5k triggered sprint at 6–8h loaded is a business. Compressing to 1.5h for the 1,000-record 80% GM model is not an 11-week result.

**Eligibility inside a 50-trade book (if you ever get one):** ~14–20 drill-passable after license/revenue/source/authority filters, then 1–3% of monthly-close encounters convert to a paid present-failure job → **~0.5–1.5 paid per partner per close cycle**. Six partners × two batches in two weeks does not produce 12 paid unless the batches are not random.

## Final recommendation

**Do not proceed with the current 11-week architecture as written.** Keep Continuity Evidence as the object. Narrow the motion. Do not change the long-term payer map (owner once at a trigger; professional recurring; transaction later).

### Do this instead (maximizes Demo Day numbers *and* the Stripe-for-M&A narrative)

1. **Stop pretending the company is zero.** Keep the $173k / two closes / under-contract file / DealIQ pilots on the scoreboard. Continuity Evidence is a new SKU, not a new company.
2. **Do not open TX/TN as the proof geography.** Run the first cohorts through the CPA and consultant channel that already sent HVAC work. Add P&C only if a named agency is already in diligence this week.
3. **Sell triggered only.** Adviser says too-early-but-fixable, lender/broker says not-ready, owner has a 6–24 month horizon, or the monthly close just surfaced a present failure. Kill every-nth as a week-1 requirement. Reintroduce randomization in October as an integrity test, not as the acquisition engine.
4. **Price at the cleared band.** $3–5k finite sprint (HVAC already paid $5k). $1,500 only as partner-sponsored or as a discount against the real SKU. Do not teach the market a $1,500 ceiling.
5. **Decouple VRO.** Week 2 success = paid + connected + reviewed + time captured. Verified repair = Sep 18 requirement. Reviewer matched-file = October. Activations = existing pipeline, labeled honestly.
6. **Replace conjunctive company-stops with layered kills** (the plan’s own backup: continuity vs TransferOS vs stop). Aug 28 is a dashboard, not a verdict.
7. **Founder time: ~70% seller/professional acquisition and delivery, not org-chart theater.** If engineers exist: evidence graph, workbench, funnel instrumentation, one export. Cap product surface. Do not staff an agent control plane or two security FTEs against a 20-file experiment.
8. **Demo Day slide:** N businesses with connected books and a source-proven, reviewer-usable record; 2–4 professionals who sent a second cohort; hours and GM told with founder labor included; **plus** the closes and under-contract file you already have. Optional: one lender who used the export twice. Do not lead with “Stripe” or with 20 VROs and $0 GMV.

### What not to do

- Do not staff a marketplace, CIM factory, or OffDeal/Eilla clone.
- Do not kill brokerage runoff to purify the experiment.
- Do not make six cold partners the load-bearing beam of the batch.
- Do not confuse a high-integrity metric (VRO) with a fundable business (reuse of the record by a professional who comes back).

**Bottom line.** Continuity Evidence + professional-first is still the right hill. This sprint plan attacks the hill with a brittle protocol, a fake zero, and a Demo Day definition that hides the only numbers you have already earned. Rewrite the eleven weeks as a triggered, warm-channel, quality-barred volume play. That is the path that can both print a partner-credible Demo Day and still compound into a transfer rail after the batch.
