## Executive summary

- **Valuation Commodity**: Sunbelt promotes a "complimentary valuation range," BizBuySell offers a "fast, free estimate of value" and a valuation report, and FE International uses "Get a Free Valuation." -> Replace the number-first promise with a transaction artifact that proves intent and exposes a next action.
- **Artifact Over Estimate**: Serious sell-side work involves normalized earnings, customer and product analysis, contracts, working capital, buyer diligence, and a defensible narrative, not just an input form. -> Sell a compact risk map, gap scan, scenario, or buyer-fit output.
- **Readiness Beats Stated Timing**: An owner who produces consistent reporting, documents add-backs, tests management independence, and protects confidentiality is more actionable than one who merely says "within 12 months." A readiness checklist recommends starting 18 months before a process, with some proof points needing 12-18 months to demonstrate. -> Score observed preparation behaviors and give a short remediation plan.
- **Five-Minute AI Is Real at First Pass**: M&A software can search a data room, summarize documents, compare a diligence request list with room contents, and flag contradictions or information gaps. Document services can extract text, tables, headings, forms, and query responses. -> Automate triage, show evidence, and reserve judgment for a human advisor.
- **Buyer Intelligence Is a Moat, Not a Claim**: Grata describes private-market AI for screening targets and building buyer lists, while SourceScrub describes 220,000-plus information sources and 16M companies. -> Make buyer-fit output the premium tier, not the first anonymous form.
- **Confidentiality Is the Risk Reversal**: Axial emphasizes private, controlled outreach with no public deal-searching, and MidCap calls confidentiality paramount. -> State no public listing, no buyer outreach, and a clear upload deletion policy before asking for sensitive data.
- **Structure Creates Intent**: Cash at close, rollover, earnout, seller note, recapitalization, and sale-to-management scenarios force a real decision without asking "what is my business worth?" -> Use the visitor's own assumptions and label outputs illustrative, not tax, legal, financing, or valuation advice.
- **PE Needs Its Own Product**: PMI names a Deal Readiness Rating methodology for sellers and describes work with PE operating partners and portfolio-company management around add-ons. -> Build sponsor-aware exit and add-on artifacts rather than putting a portfolio company through a founder-retirement quiz.

## Evidence: why free valuation is the anti-pattern

A free valuation is not inherently useless. It is a poor primary promise for this audience because it rewards the least informative behavior: entering a few facts to receive a number. That creates a structural tire-kicker channel. The reviewed pages do not publish a controlled conversion study proving that free valuation produces worse results, so the tire-kicker conclusion is a funnel-design inference, not a measured benchmark.

The credibility problem is methodological. A quality-of-earnings analysis establishes normalized performance by adjusting one-time events, personal expenses, GAAP-related items, customers, product lines, and cash receipts against reported numbers. MidCap says its advisors dig into the general ledger to normalize financials and surface true drivers. Founders Advisors says add-backs must be identified and defended, including personal expenses and operations that will not continue after the transaction. A form-driven multiple cannot see those distinctions.

The diligence mismatch is also material. Buyers review contracts, transferability, KPIs, projections, working capital, management depth, and operational risk before they trust the earnings story. A seller can therefore receive a plausible-looking estimate and still encounter a large reset after the buyer examines add-backs, recurring revenue, churn, owner compensation, or customer behavior. An M&A attorney's account of failed deals describes EBITDA analysis that does not hold up, aggressive add-backs, and revenue that turns out not to be recurring. Axial's broken-LOI report separately identifies QoE EBITDA discrepancies in 21.3% of broken processes. | Free-valuation promise | Serious-seller reality | Better landing artifact |
|---|---|---|
| One instant number | Normalized earnings require transaction-specific adjustments and customer or product analysis. | Add-Back Defensibility Scan with evidence requests |
| Minimal form completion | Readiness is visible in reporting consistency, management independence, contracts, and documentation. | Deal-Ready Signal Map |
| Generic market multiple | A buyer needs a credible teaser, NDA-gated CIM, IOI support, and later diligence consistency. | CIM Gap Scan or Teaser Safety Check |
| Seller curiosity | Structure choices reveal whether the person is deciding on a sale, recap, rollover, earnout, or financing path. | Structure Explorer or Proceeds Scenario |

The design conclusion is not to hide valuation forever. It is to move valuation downstream, after the visitor has unlocked a more credible artifact and supplied signals that make an advisor conversation useful.

|---|---|---|

| Earnings defensibility | Add-backs are documented under a written policy and can be defended; projections exist. | The visitor is anticipating QoE and buyer questions. |
| Management transfer | The senior team has run the business without the founder in recurring decisions for six or more months. | The owner is testing a real value driver, not only answering a survey. |

The transaction artifacts explain why this is a product opportunity. A teaser is a one-to-two-page anonymous entry point distributed to a broad buyer list; a CIM follows NDA execution and gives qualified buyers the detail needed to submit a credible IOI. The teaser is limited while a CIM can be 30-80 or more pages and is intended for qualified buyers. A landing page can let a visitor self-check one slice of that process without pretending to deliver a full CIM.

A sell-side QoE is a useful benchmark for seriousness but not the right free offer: Greenwich says it produces a normalized financial picture, helps identify and fix diligence issues, can accelerate a sale, and may reduce time in market, while stating that LMM sellers can typically expect roughly $40,000-$200,000 for the full analysis. The productized offer should therefore be a triage screen, not a free substitute for a QoE.



## Evidence: what AI can really do in 2026

The production boundary is first-pass compression. Datasite describes natural-language questions across a data room, summaries, diligence-request-list comparison, contradiction and gap flags, file-difference analysis, and suggested clarifications. Microsoft describes a confidence score as the probability that an extracted result was detected correctly, and its layout model extracts text, tables, selections, titles, section headings, headers, and footers. AWS describes extraction of text, forms, tables, query responses, and signatures. | AI capability | Real five-minute implementation | Boundary to disclose |
|---|---|---|
| PDF and CSV ingestion | Extract tables, headings, cells, dates, and labels; map them into a normalized review schema. | A confidence score measures detection probability, not truth, materiality, or accounting judgment. |
| Diligence gap detection | Compare an uploaded checklist, teaser, or mini data room against expected sections and flag missing or inconsistent evidence. Datasite describes this workflow directly. | A gap flag is a review queue, not a legal conclusion or a complete diligence opinion. |
| Private-market matching | Map NAICS or industry, geography, size, ownership, acquisition thesis, and buyer type against licensed private-company data. Grata and SourceScrub demonstrate that the data and search category exists. | A match is not a buyer mandate, willingness to pay, financing approval, or probability of closing. |
| Pattern scoring | Calculate add-back documentation coverage, customer concentration bands, KPI completeness, owner-dependence answers, and process-risk scores. | The score is a repeatable screening heuristic whose weights should be visible and reviewable. |
| Scenario math | Run deterministic cash, rollover, earnout, seller-note, escrow, debt-payoff, and financing cases from user-supplied assumptions. | The formula engine is not a valuation, tax opinion, legal opinion, lender commitment, or prediction of buyer acceptance. |
| Confidential document workflow | Encrypt, restrict access, show retention and deletion terms, and avoid public outreach. Datasite describes security and regulatory-compliance framing; Azure states that models are stateless but prompts and generated content may be stored in the customer's Azure geography. | A vendor's security statement is not the firm's own legal promise. Document the actual processor, location, retention, subprocessors, and deletion behavior. |
| Source-grounded explanations | Return page, row, or field references beside flags so an advisor can inspect the evidence. | Never imply that a generated narrative is independently verified. |

Private-market intelligence is real, but data quality remains a dependency. SourceScrub explicitly says high-quality data and content are needed for useful AI and describes human-enriched labeling and analyst teams. Cactus, in an adjacent CRE workflow, claims that users can upload financials, rent rolls, and offering memoranda, parse them, benchmark live comps, and produce DCF analyses in minutes. That is evidence that the upload-to-analysis interaction is technically marketable, not proof that an M&A advisor can produce a reliable valuation in five minutes.

Two claims should remain out of bounds: "AI determined your business value" and "AI found the buyer who will buy you." The first ignores normalization, quality of revenue, and diligence. The second confuses database similarity with a live mandate. Use AI for extraction, classification, comparison, and transparent triage; use people for materiality, judgment, outreach permission, tax, legal, accounting, and negotiation.

## Productized offers

The offers below intentionally avoid free valuation, instant multiple, and public-listing language. Each is designed for a 2-5 minute browser experience, with a result that can be repeated across sectors and handed to an advisor without turning the advisor into a custom-report factory.



## No-upload diagnostics and process control

These products maximize privacy and conversion. They qualify through decisions and behaviors rather than asking an owner to upload a tax return before trust exists. Their mechanics reflect the preparation, confidentiality, and teaser/CIM evidence above. ### 1. Deal-Ready Signal Map

1. **Offer name:** Deal-Ready Signal Map
2. **One-line hook:** "See the six buyer-friction signals to fix before you invite anyone into the process."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A Deal Readiness Score from 0-100 across reporting hygiene, earnings defensibility, management independence, customer and revenue visibility, legal/document readiness, and process control; three red flags; three strengths; and a 90-day action sequence. The output is a browser card with an optional one-page download, not a valuation.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** It attracts an owner who is willing to disclose how the company operates and what has already been prepared. It gives the visitor a useful answer even if the correct decision is "prepare for another six months," which is more credible than a flattering number.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** A branching model can adapt questions to founder-led, PE-backed, service, manufacturing, or recurring-revenue businesses, normalize answers into comparable risk dimensions, and explain why a response changed the score. A human can advise, but cannot consistently synthesize hundreds of short assessments into a sector-specific action taxonomy at landing-page speed.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; founder-led or PE-backed; revenue band; EBITDA or SDE band; employee band; target window; preferred transaction type; monthly reporting status; owner involvement in recurring decisions. Optional: sector/NAICS, geography, customer concentration band, and whether a QoE has been completed.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Readiness score, actual transaction window, business scale, deal type, management depth, and whether the visitor chooses "fix first" or "speak with an advisor."
8. **Natural next step:** Offer a confidential 20-minute review of the three red flags, with no buyer outreach and no public listing. If the score is low, route to a preparation sprint; if high, route to CIM, buyer-fit, or process design.

### 2. Founder Independence Test

1. **Offer name:** Founder Independence Test
2. **One-line hook:** "Would the business keep executing if you were unavailable for six months?"
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** An Owner-Dependence Score from 0-100, a decision map covering sales, customer relationships, approvals, hiring, pricing, operations, and finance, plus a six-month independence experiment with named owners and metrics.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** Owner dependence is a real deal question and the result is actionable before a sale. It also attracts an owner thinking about transferability, management succession, or sale-to-management rather than a visitor hunting for a headline multiple.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** The engine turns free-text examples into recurring decision categories, detects contradictions between "the team runs it" and the listed approvals only the founder can make, and generates a role-specific test. It can compare patterns across hundreds of anonymized responses without exposing any company's identity.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; employees; founder hours per week; decisions requiring founder approval; top customer relationships; senior-team tenure; target window; desired role after closing. Optional: organization chart upload with names removed.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Owner dependency, management depth, transition preference, and willingness to run a real pre-sale experiment. A founder who supplies named managers and metrics is a higher-intent lead than one who only selects a timeline.
8. **Natural next step:** A management-transfer review that converts the six-month experiment into a buyer-ready management narrative. Founders Advisors recommends capable teams, documented systems, and productive teams as preparation areas. ### 3. Confidential Process and Leak-Risk Planner

1. **Offer name:** Confidential Process and Leak-Risk Planner
2. **One-line hook:** "Plan a sale without telling the market, your team, or your customers too soon."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A Leak-Risk Score from 0-100, a risk map for employees, customers, suppliers, competitors, and local-market clues, a recommended contact sequence, a teaser anonymity checklist, and a list of people who should not be contacted until a specified stage.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** Confidentiality is an immediate and emotional owner problem. The artifact helps a serious seller understand process design before making an irreversible disclosure, and it makes the firm's discretion visible without implying that the business is already for sale.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI can combine the visitor's industry uniqueness, customer concentration, employee awareness, geography, and channel choices into a consistent risk tree; it can identify combinations that make an anonymous teaser identifiable and generate safer alternatives. The score is a planning heuristic, not a guarantee of secrecy.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Industry; geography; employee count; number of customers; top-customer concentration band; whether employees know; whether competitors are obvious; target window; desired buyer types. Optional: redacted teaser or a list of sensitive relationships.
7. **Lead quality signal:** The visitor's sensitivity to leaks, concentration risk, process timing, preferred buyer controls, and willingness to use NDA-gated outreach. It also reveals whether a strategic sale or a financial-buyer process is more plausible.
8. **Natural next step:** A confidential process-design session that defines teaser approval, NDA gates, buyer-contact permissions, and an internal communication plan. Axial emphasizes seller control and no public deal-searching, while MidCap calls confidentiality paramount. ### 4. Structure Preference Brief: Sale, Recap, or Management Transition

1. **Offer name:** Structure Preference Brief
2. **One-line hook:** "Decide what outcome you actually want before buyers decide it for you."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A three-route brief comparing majority sale, minority recapitalization, and sale-to-management across control, liquidity, ongoing role, financing complexity, rollover, and timing. It ends with the visitor's top two unresolved terms and a list of questions for an advisor.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** A structure decision is closer to the real mandate. It qualifies whether the visitor wants liquidity, growth capital, succession, control retention, or a clean exit, without asking for a long sales call or pretending the answer is a single price.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI can translate a short narrative such as "I want liquidity but still want upside" into a structured preference tree, detect incompatible choices, and show which missing facts would change the route. The underlying comparisons should be deterministic and transparent; AI should not choose a structure for the visitor.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Founder or sponsor; desired liquidity percentage; desired post-close role; control preference; growth-capital need; target window; transaction type under consideration. Optional: debt band, management successor, rollover appetite, and known buyer interest.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Actual objective, control and liquidity preference, transaction type, and whether the visitor is open to minority equity or sale-to-management. This is a stronger mandate signal than a request for an estimate.
8. **Natural next step:** A structure workshop with M&A counsel and tax advisors as needed. CBIZ identifies equity rollover planning and post-close structuring among transaction-advisory work, which makes the handoff concrete rather than generic.



## Light-upload financial and document artifacts

These tools use the AI advantage where it is most defensible: extracting tables, comparing evidence, and creating a review queue. They should accept one small PDF or CSV first, show confidence or source references, and allow a no-upload path. The full QoE remains a paid professional engagement; Greenwich describes its depth and cost separately. ### 5. Add-Back Defensibility Scan

1. **Offer name:** Add-Back Defensibility Scan
2. **One-line hook:** "Find the EBITDA adjustments a buyer is most likely to question before QoE."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A normalized EBITDA bridge from the uploaded period, with each adjustment labeled "likely defensible," "needs documentation," or "buyer challenge." The result lists the source row or page, missing support, recurring-versus-one-time questions, and a documentation checklist. It explicitly says "screening review, not a QoE or valuation."
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** It addresses a known post-LOI failure point and gives the owner a way to improve the earnings story. It attracts a seller who has a P&L, knows what an add-back is, or is willing to gather support, rather than someone seeking a number from a form.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** Document AI extracts tables and labels; a classification layer recognizes owner compensation, personal expenses, one-time events, unusual professional fees, and discontinued operations; a rules engine asks for evidence. A human can review the exceptions, but cannot manually normalize hundreds of first-pass files at the same speed.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; founder-led or PE-backed; revenue and EBITDA bands; target window; country/state; one P&L PDF or CSV for one to three periods. Optional: general-ledger export, add-back schedule, tax return pages, and support documents with names and account numbers redacted.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Usable financial data, EBITDA scale, number and size of adjustments, documentation maturity, sector, timeline, and willingness to address challenged items. A large unsupported adjustment is both a quality signal and a coaching opportunity.
8. **Natural next step:** Send the exception list to an advisor for a scoped sell-side QoE readiness review. Do not promise that an adjustment will be accepted; the source material says QoE identifies and adjusts items to establish normalized performance, which is a professional judgment exercise. Founders also emphasizes defending add-backs in detail. ### 6. Revenue Durability and Concentration Map

1. **Offer name:** Revenue Durability and Concentration Map
2. **One-line hook:** "Show where revenue is durable, exposed, or dependent on too few relationships."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A browser chart based on an anonymized customer CSV: top-one, top-five, and top-ten concentration bands; period-over-period movement; recurring, contracted, project, or unknown revenue labels; churn or reactivation flags when dates are supplied; and five buyer questions to answer before a process.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** Revenue quality changes how a buyer interprets earnings, growth, and risk. The output helps an owner protect customer identities while confronting concentration and recurring-revenue questions that a generic multiple cannot see.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI maps inconsistent customer IDs, identifies missing periods, classifies contract and revenue descriptors, and explains which rows drive a concentration flag. It can preserve anonymization while turning a messy export into a reviewable pattern. It must not infer retention, contractability, or customer sentiment from absent data.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; industry; revenue band; target window; customer ID; period; revenue; optional start/end date and revenue type. Customer names, email addresses, and account numbers are prohibited by default. Optional: geography, vertical, contract term, and gross margin.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Data availability, concentration bands, revenue durability, sector, timing, and whether the owner has contracts or cohort information. The visitor's willingness to upload an anonymized file is a strong intent signal without exposing identity.
8. **Natural next step:** A revenue-quality and customer-risk review that produces a safe teaser narrative and a diligence request list. CBIZ says sell-side readiness includes highlighting KPIs, agreements, projections, and high-risk areas early. ### 7. CIM Gap and Consistency Scan

1. **Offer name:** CIM Gap and Consistency Scan
2. **One-line hook:** "Before a buyer reads your CIM, see the six sections and numbers most likely to trigger follow-up."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A CIM Gap Score from 0-100, the six missing or thin sections, a cross-document consistency list, five likely buyer follow-ups, and a redaction checklist. If the visitor uploads only a teaser, the output switches to a teaser-to-CIM readiness mode rather than pretending to review a full CIM.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** A CIM is a real transaction artifact. The result is useful even when the company is not ready to market and creates a credible bridge to preparation, unlike a number that may reset as soon as diligence begins.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI classifies sections, extracts financial ranges, compares the narrative with supplied KPIs, detects missing ownership or working-capital discussion, and flags uniquely identifying clues. It can review a 1-20 page upload in minutes; a person should validate materiality and language.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; company stage; revenue and EBITDA bands; target window; transaction objective; one teaser or CIM PDF. Optional: financial-summary CSV, requested buyer types, and a redacted diligence request list.
7. **Lead quality signal:** The existence and maturity of marketing materials, whether an NDA process exists, document completeness, target timing, and whether the visitor has already received buyer questions or IOIs.
8. **Natural next step:** A paid or scoped CIM-readiness review that fixes the highest-impact gaps without generating a generic 40-page report. A teaser is anonymous and typically one to two pages, while a CIM follows NDA execution and supports an IOI. ### 8. Working Capital and Closing-Adjustment Scan

1. **Offer name:** Working Capital and Closing-Adjustment Scan
2. **One-line hook:** "See the balance-sheet questions that can change a deal after the headline terms are agreed."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A Working-Capital Readiness Score from 0-100, a trailing monthly bridge for current assets and liabilities, seasonality and aged-receivable flags, inventory or prepaid anomalies, debt-like-item prompts, and a list of questions to define a normalized peg. It does not recommend a peg or predict a price adjustment.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** It addresses a closing mechanic that can change cash proceeds and cause friction after an LOI. It attracts a finance-literate owner or deal lead who has balance-sheet data and understands that enterprise value is not the same as cash received.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI maps inconsistent chart-of-accounts labels, parses monthly statements, detects seasonality, and highlights periods that need human explanation. The output can cite the row and month that caused each flag, making it auditable.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; business type; target window; revenue band; one monthly balance-sheet CSV or PDF; optional AR aging and inventory report. Optional: current working-capital policy and any buyer-proposed peg language, redacted.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Availability of monthly balance-sheet data, seasonality, potential adjustment complexity, transaction stage, and whether the visitor already has an LOI or buyer request.
8. **Natural next step:** A QoE and net-working-capital scoping call with an advisor and accounting specialist. Financial information and working-capital considerations are explicitly part of CIM content, and CBIZ lists working-capital modeling among its diligence work.



## Structure and financing scenarios

Scenario tools should never reverse-engineer a business value from thin inputs. They should either use a price or range supplied by the visitor, or let the visitor select clearly labeled illustrative assumptions. The output should show mechanics and trade-offs, not advice. This is consistent with the market's emphasis on transaction alternatives, rollover, and financial investors. ### 9. Cash, Rollover, Earnout, and Seller-Note Explorer

1. **Offer name:** Cash, Rollover, Earnout, and Seller-Note Explorer
2. **One-line hook:** "Compare certainty today with upside, control, and risk after closing."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A side-by-side waterfall for cash at close, rollover equity, earnout, seller note, escrow, and holdback; a certainty-versus-upside matrix; and sensitivity cases for earnout achievement, note repayment, and rollover value. It begins from a user-supplied offer or illustrative price range and never generates a company valuation.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** A real seller often chooses between liquidity and continued upside. The tool reveals risk tolerance, desired role, willingness to roll equity, and whether an earnout is acceptable, all of which make the next advisory conversation more substantive.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI converts plain-English objectives into a structured scenario, detects missing terms, translates an LOI clause into model inputs, and explains what changes the waterfall. The cash-flow math remains deterministic and visible; AI does not forecast buyer behavior or legal enforceability.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Role; known offer or illustrative price range; debt payoff; cash at close; rollover percentage; earnout amount and conditions; seller-note terms; escrow or holdback; desired post-close role. Optional: redacted LOI and state/country for professional referral routing.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Whether there is already an offer, the stage from IOI to LOI, preferred liquidity, structure complexity, and readiness to discuss a transaction rather than a hypothetical value.
8. **Natural next step:** An advisor reviews the assumptions, then coordinates tax and legal counsel for definitive terms. The page must state that the output is not tax, legal, accounting, or valuation advice and that buyers may reject any structure.

### 10. After-Tax Proceeds and Liquidity Planner

1. **Offer name:** After-Tax Proceeds and Liquidity Planner
2. **One-line hook:** "Translate a proposed deal into the liquidity you may actually keep - without guessing your business value."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A proceeds waterfall that starts with the visitor's own proposed price or offer, subtracts debt, fees, escrow, earnout deferral, and rollover, and displays low, base, and high after-tax placeholders using user-selected assumptions. It shows which assumptions require a CPA or tax attorney.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** The visitor may not need another estimate; they may need to know whether an offer supports a personal, family, or reinvestment objective. The tool also identifies people who already have an IOI, LOI, or term sheet.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI can read a redacted term sheet, map consideration components, detect missing tax-sensitive terms, and ask only the follow-up questions relevant to asset versus equity sale, rollover, escrow, or earnout. Formula outputs must be displayed beside source terms and assumptions.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Country; state or province; entity type; user-supplied offer or price range; debt; transaction expenses; escrow; earnout; rollover; desired liquidity; selected illustrative tax assumptions. Optional: redacted LOI and CPA-prepared assumptions.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Offer stage, deal structure, liquidity objective, cross-border flag, and need for tax-aware transaction design. It can also identify when the visitor is not ready because no transaction assumption exists.
8. **Natural next step:** A confidential proceeds review with the advisor and the visitor's tax counsel. The page must say "illustrative planning only," not tax advice, and must not imply a formal valuation or tax result.

### 11. SBA and Searcher-Financing Feasibility Screen

1. **Offer name:** SBA and Searcher-Financing Feasibility Screen
2. **One-line hook:** "See whether the deal shape is compatible with an acquisition-financing conversation before you spend weeks on diligence."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A financing-path screen comparing cash, SBA 7(a), seller note, searcher equity, and a blended structure; a simple debt-service coverage proxy; the five missing lender documents; and a result of "potentially financeable," "needs assumptions," or "not enough information." The SBA itself lists buying an existing business or franchise as a 7(a) use. 4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** It qualifies both seller and buyer seriousness around a feasible capital stack. A seller learns whether a likely buyer universe can support the structure; a searcher learns what to prepare without receiving a false lender approval.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI maps business type, cash-flow band, buyer equity, seller financing, and ownership role to a transparent rule set, extracts requested figures from a redacted financial PDF, and identifies missing evidence. It does not underwrite credit or speak for a lender.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Visitor role; purchase price or transaction range supplied by the user; cash equity; seller-note willingness; TTM cash flow or EBITDA band; existing debt; buyer operating experience; business type; geography. Optional: tax returns, P&L, debt schedule, franchise information, and lender checklist.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Buyer type, financing need, equity availability, debt capacity proxy, operating experience, deal stage, and whether the seller is willing to consider seller financing.
8. **Natural next step:** Route to an acquisition-finance partner or advisor for a lender-specific screen. State clearly that the result is not SBA eligibility, lender prequalification, credit approval, tax advice, or a valuation.



## Buyer intelligence and PE-specific offers

These offers have the strongest brand differentiation but require licensed data, careful permissions, and a no-outreach default. Axial describes controlled buyer selection and confidential distribution, while Grata and SourceScrub demonstrate the market for AI-assisted private-company discovery. ### 12. Buyer Universe Fit Map

1. **Offer name:** Buyer Universe Fit Map
2. **One-line hook:** "See which buyer types fit your deal before your identity leaves the room."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** An anonymized buyer-fit matrix showing likely strategic, PE add-on, independent-sponsor, search-fund, family-office, and SBA-backed archetypes; a sector and geography fit score; an estimated concentration of fit by archetype; and a process recommendation of broad, targeted, or invitation-only outreach. It shows categories and reasons, not names or outreach.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** Buyers and process design determine whether an outcome is competitive and confidential. The artifact tells an owner what kind of process may fit, reveals trade-offs between breadth and discretion, and creates value even before a mandate is signed.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** A buyer graph can combine NAICS or sector, revenue, EBITDA, geography, ownership, acquisition history, stated criteria, and portfolio adjacency. AI can rank and explain overlap across a large private-market corpus, while a human validates the candidates and obtains permission before contact. Grata describes buyer-list screening and live mandates; Axial describes controlled buyer selection. 6. **Minimum capture fields:** Industry or NAICS; revenue and EBITDA bands; geography; customer and revenue model; desired transaction type; target window; buyer types allowed; confidentiality exclusions. Optional: anonymized teaser, known competitors, ownership type, acquisition constraints, and preferred post-close role.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Strategic fit, business scale, buyer-type preference, confidentiality sensitivity, timeline, and whether the visitor can articulate a transaction thesis. It also reveals whether a buyer graph is likely to add value or whether preparation should come first.
8. **Natural next step:** Offer a permissioned buyer-universe review. The advisor can validate the top archetypes, remove conflicted or prohibited names, and obtain written approval before any teaser or NDA is sent. No public listing and no automatic outreach.

### 13. PE Portfolio Exit Readiness Board

1. **Offer name:** PE Portfolio Exit Readiness Board
2. **One-line hook:** "Give the sponsor and management team a board-ready view of what could slow the next exit."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A one-page Portfolio Exit Readiness Board with a 0-100 score, six exception lanes - QoE, KPI consistency, management depth, customer and revenue quality, legal/data-room readiness, and buyer narrative - plus a 90-day remediation backlog and a suggested exit route to discuss. A KPI CSV is optional.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** A sponsor already has a value thesis or investment model. The higher-value question is whether the portfolio company can defend it under diligence and whether management can execute the process without distracting operations. The output is suitable for an operating-partner conversation, not a founder curiosity funnel.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** AI can ingest a small KPI export or diligence checklist, compare current reporting to the stated exit story, flag missing evidence, and separate platform-company risks from add-on integration risks. It can create repeatable exception reporting across a portfolio, while an advisor makes the board-level judgment.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Sponsor, operating partner, CEO, or CFO role; platform or add-on; revenue and EBITDA bands; target window; last QoE status; monthly KPI status; management independence; data-room status; intended exit or recap route. Optional: anonymized KPI CSV, IC memo, or diligence request list.
7. **Lead quality signal:** PE ownership, role seniority, portfolio scale, exit or recap window, reporting maturity, QoE status, management depth, and whether the sponsor will fund remediation. This is a very high-quality signal even when the visitor is not ready to launch.
8. **Natural next step:** A sponsor and management working session that converts the exceptions into a sell-side readiness plan, data-room sequence, and board update. PMI's Deal Readiness Rating uses initial assessment, value creation, and risk mitigation, and PMI describes working with PE operating partners and portfolio-company management. ### 14. Add-On, Recap, or Exit Route Selector for PE Portfolios

1. **Offer name:** Add-On, Recap, or Exit Route Selector
2. **One-line hook:** "Compare the next capital event against your portfolio strategy, integration capacity, and liquidity goal."
3. **What they get in 2-5 minutes:** A route matrix comparing a strategic add-on, sponsor-to-sponsor sale, minority recap, full sale, and sale-to-management across strategic fit, integration load, capital need, rollover, management bandwidth, and confidentiality. It returns the top two routes, the assumptions driving the result, and a short IC discussion agenda.
4. **Why this is better than a free valuation:** PE deal leads need a portfolio decision, not a generic owner estimate. The artifact recognizes that an add-on can create portfolio complexity and personnel-integration challenges, while a recap or exit changes liquidity and control. It qualifies the sponsor's actual mandate.
5. **How AI makes it uniquely good:** A buyer graph can compare the platform with acquisition candidates and strategics; document AI can extract portfolio constraints from an IC memo; a rules engine can score integration and capital requirements. It can do this consistently across companies, but it cannot establish synergies, approve a deal, or predict a buyer's IC vote.
6. **Minimum capture fields:** Sponsor or portfolio role; platform or add-on; revenue and EBITDA bands; target window; route options; integration capacity; management bandwidth; desired liquidity; rollover preference; geography. Optional: anonymized portfolio table, acquisition criteria, IC memo, or buyer list.
7. **Lead quality signal:** Route urgency, portfolio strategy, capital need, integration readiness, target size, buyer universe, and sponsor decision stage. The response separates an active mandate from an exploratory portfolio exercise.
8. **Natural next step:** A route-validation meeting with the sponsor, management, and advisor, followed by permissioned buyer or target mapping. PMI identifies portfolio complexity, personnel integration, and portfolio-strategy selection as PE add-on challenges.

### Top 3 I Would Ship First

**1. Deal-Ready Signal Map - S, no upload.** This is the fastest path to a credible replacement for free valuation. It can launch with a transparent rules engine, six dimensions, and a clean browser result. It should convert better than a file-upload tool because the owner can remain anonymous until the result is useful. Its brand value comes from saying, "We will tell you what needs work," rather than flattering the visitor with a number.

**2. Add-Back Defensibility Scan - M, light document parse.** This is the best AI-native proof point. A single P&L or CSV lets the firm demonstrate extraction, classification, source references, and a buyer-relevant exception list. It should produce fewer completions than the Signal Map, but the leads will reveal financial scale, data maturity, and likely QoE friction. **3. Buyer Universe Fit Map - L, buyer graph.** This is the clearest premium differentiator and the hardest to build. ## Offers to avoid

- **Free company valuation, instant multiple, or "what is my business worth?"** These are the commoditized front door documented above. They invite curiosity, imply false precision, and hide the normalized-earnings, customer, contract, and structure work that real buyers perform.
- **"Sell your business in 30 days" or any guaranteed-close promise.** It rewards speed over preparation, conflicts with the time required to demonstrate management independence and reporting quality, and damages a premium brand when diligence takes longer.
- **Public listing teases or automatic buyer blasts.** A teaser should be anonymous and further information should follow NDA; Axial emphasizes controlled, non-public distribution. A landing page must not imply that the business is for sale without explicit consent.
- **A 40-page PDF report.** It feels like gated content, not an artifact. Return a compact score, exception list, bridge, or scenario in the browser, with an optional one-page export.
- **Personality quizzes and generic AI scores.** If the score does not cite an input, explain a mechanism, and produce a next action, it is a gimmick and will undermine data-forward positioning.
- **A free full QoE, formal valuation, tax opinion, legal review, or lender prequalification.** These require professional judgment, engagement scope, or third-party approval. Offer triage and evidence requests, then hand off cleanly.
- **Unbounded sensitive uploads.** Do not ask for unredacted tax returns, customer names, SSNs, bank details, passwords, or employee data at the first touch. State permitted file types, encryption, retention, deletion, subprocessors, geography, and whether data is used to train models.
- **A long sales form before value.** Ask for only fields that change the artifact. Let the visitor select a role, objective, scale band, and timing, then invite optional detail after the result.



## Positioning and operating guardrails

The page should sound like a discreet transaction desk, not a broker directory. A strong headline is: "Know what a buyer will challenge before you invite one in." A supporting line is: "A confidential five-minute diagnostic of readiness, diligence friction, and process risk - not a valuation calculator and not a public listing."

Use operational vocabulary sparingly but confidently: normalized EBITDA, add-back support, customer concentration, owner dependence, teaser, CIM, NDA, IOI, LOI, working-capital peg, rollover, earnout, reps and warranties, SBA feasibility, and data-room gaps. The vocabulary signals that the artifact is built around a real process. It must be paired with plain explanations so a Main Street owner is not punished for lacking deal experience.

Proof should be visible inside the result. Show the inputs used, the scoring dimensions, the evidence row or document page behind each flag, the date of the benchmark or data refresh where relevant, and a confidence or "needs review" label. Do not claim that a proprietary AI found the perfect buyer or determined fair market value. The user should be able to understand why the output changed when an input changed.

Risk reversal should appear before the form: no public listing; no buyer outreach; no contact with employees, customers, suppliers, or competitors; confidential handling; delete uploaded files after a stated period; and use only anonymized customer data. If those promises are not operationally true, do not publish them. Azure's privacy description illustrates why a vendor-level statement needs qualifiers around geography and retention, even when models are described as stateless. Compliance copy should say, as applicable: "This is an indicative screening artifact, not a formal business valuation, QoE, accounting opinion, tax advice, legal advice, lender decision, or buyer commitment." For tax-sensitive outputs, say that tax treatment depends on facts, entity, jurisdiction, asset-versus-equity structure, and professional advice. For SBA output, say that only a lender or the SBA process can determine eligibility. For buyer maps, say that a match is not an expression of interest. For every upload, state the accepted file types, redaction requirements, access controls, retention and deletion rules, and whether the firm will use the file to improve models.

The call to action should be a choice, not a trap: "Unlock your Signal Map," followed by "Review the three flags privately," "Run the document scan," or "Ask for a confidential advisor review." The result should never automatically create a listing, notify a buyer, or imply consent to market the company.



## Synthesis

The fourteen offers form a portfolio of different mechanisms rather than fourteen versions of an assessment.

| Offer family | Primary audience | Core mechanism | AI depth | Conversion and quality trade-off | Best time horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No-upload diagnostics | Founder-led owners | Behavioral evidence and decision choices | Rules, branching, text classification | Highest conversion, moderate-to-high quality, lowest privacy risk | 12-24 months |
| Light-upload financial tools | Owners, CFOs, deal leads | Extract, reconcile, and flag evidence | Document AI, table mapping, anomaly triage | More friction, much higher quality, highest sensitive-data risk | 6-18 months |
| Structure scenarios | Owners with a goal, offer, or term sheet | Deterministic trade-off and proceeds math | Clause extraction plus formula engine | Good conversion when a live decision exists; advice risk must be controlled | 0-12 months or active process |
| Buyer Universe Fit Map | Owners and advisors | Graph overlap and controlled process design | Private-market data, entity resolution, ranking | Lower conversion, strongest differentiation, data-licensing and confidentiality burden | 6-24 months |
| PE portfolio tools | Sponsors, operating partners, portfolio CFOs | Exception reporting and route selection | Portfolio-scale comparison, KPI and document analysis | Fewer users, highest lead value, sponsor-specific privacy and workflow needs | 6-24 months |

The first tension is conversion versus moat. No-upload tools are easier to trust and easier to ship, but their AI advantage is mostly in consistent synthesis. Light-upload tools demonstrate the unfair capability more visibly, yet they require a real privacy program and better error handling. Buyer graphs offer the strongest premium story, but a thin or stale data layer will be more damaging than no graph at all.

The second tension is owner simplicity versus deal realism. An owner wants a fast answer, while a buyer wants evidence across normalized earnings, contracts, management, working capital, and customer quality. The right compromise is not to hide complexity in a black-box score. It is to expose one compact slice of the process, show the evidence behind it, and offer a next step that expands scope only with permission.

The third tension is confidentiality versus qualification. Asking nothing produces weak signals; asking for names and full tax returns creates unacceptable trust friction. Anonymized customer CSVs, redacted PDFs, scale bands, role, transaction objective, and timing provide a middle path. Axial's controlled distribution model and the teaser's anonymous, NDA-gated sequence show the process principle: information should move in stages, with seller control. The fourth tension is founder-led versus PE-backed intent. A founder tool should reveal owner dependence, successor depth, liquidity, and personal objectives. A PE tool should reveal KPI consistency, QoE status, board deadlines, add-on integration capacity, portfolio strategy, and capital-event route. PMI's separation of seller readiness from PE operating-partner and add-on work supports this product split. The strategic conclusion is to build a signal engine, not a valuation funnel. The visitor first unlocks a private artifact, then chooses whether to disclose a document, a scenario, or a buyer constraint. The firm earns the right to discuss valuation only after it has shown that it can see the same risks, evidence, and process decisions that a serious buyer will see.
