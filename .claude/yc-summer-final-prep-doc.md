Scorta — YC Interview Prep (Final)
10-minute interview. Both founders speak. Conversational, not pitched. The three things YC wants to get across: problem (what is it), solution (is it good, are you the team), how big can it be. Earnestness over polish. If you don't know, say so.
1. The Two-Teams Framing
This is the architectural frame that runs through every answer. Internalize it.
Team 1 — Prep agents: Ingestion, Recast, Owner-Dependency, Concentration. Turn an unstructured main street business into a sellable asset one. Two-to-four-week cycle. Owner pays $3-5K flat.
Team 2 — Market agents: Case Manager, Boardroom, Lender Ops, Outreach. Build the deal room, run curated outreach to capital-verified buyers, broker the close. 5% success fee on sale.
When in doubt, return to this. Every product question, every "what do you actually do" question maps to one of the two teams.




3. Interview Answers (Q1–Q12)
Q1. What is your company working on?
Opening (~30 seconds):
Scorta is replacing the small business broker. We work with owners of $500K to $2M businesses and we run the entire sale process with two teams of AI agents instead of a team of people. The first team turns an unstructured business into a sellable asset — recasting financials, mapping owner dependency, pre-qualifying it with SBA lenders. The second team builds the deal room, lists on marketplaces and runs coordinated outreach to capital-verified buyers in the right industry and size segment to broker the close. Owners pay $3k to $5K flat for the first team, and a 5% success fee when the business sells.
If they ask "say more":
70 to 80% of these businesses never sell. Not because nobody wants to buy them — because nobody fixes the financials, the owner dependency, the SBA lendability, and the dozen other things that determine whether the deal closes. Traditional brokers just list and hope. Our first agent team spends two weeks to a month getting the business ready. Our two marketing agency clients went through this and saw a 20% valuation lift going into the market.
The interesting detail:
The thing most people don't know about this market: SBA underwriters reject the majority of failed Main Street deals at month four of diligence because the seller's books don't reconcile. The fix is technical — you have to recast the P&L the way an SBA underwriter would, document every add-back, defend the methodology. Traditional brokers don't do it because the labor isn't justified at our deal size. Our Recast Agent does it in 14 days. One operator at Scorta runs 20 deals where a traditional broker shop needs four to six people.
Q2. Where do new users come from?
All 9 paying customers came inbound. The 3 HVAC clients came through one CPA referral — he saw our work on his first referral and sent us two more within a week. The 2 marketing agencies came through agency-owner word of mouth after we placed the first one. The restaurants and the jewelry brand came in directly to our site after running ExitIQ.
The flow is consistent. Owners run ExitIQ — our intelligent assessment that produces a written GAP Report with valuation, buyer-archetype matching, and a 90-day plan. The GAP Report signals real expertise, which converts to a founder call. On the call we walk them through what we'll deliver over the two-to-four-week process. About 40% of inbound conversations convert to a paid Diagnostic engagement.
Going forward, CPAs are the channel. The HVAC referral pattern is the template — one CPA who trusts us is structurally worth twenty individual seller referrals because owners sell their business once in a lifetime but CPAs see five to ten exit conversations a year. Same dynamic applies to SBA loan officers, whose workload doubled after SOP 50 10 8 changed the rules in June 2025. We're rolling out white-label ExitIQ for both groups — they get co-branded assessments to send their clients, we get the engagement.
Q3. What is your growth like?
$3K in March, $8K in April, $24K in May — so roughly 2.7x then 3x month-over-month. Customer count went from 2 to 5 to 9. Off a small base, so the percentage growth is a directional signal, not statistical proof. The number we trust more is conversion: 2 of our 9 customers have signed brokerage agreements at 5% success fees, both marketing agencies, both SBA pre-qualified. Three more are in active prep work and likely to sign within 60 days.
Q4. How much are users using the product, and do they stick around?
This isn't a SaaS product so "usage" is the wrong frame. The right frame is engagement depth. Once a customer signs the Diagnostic, they're with us for two to four weeks of active prep work — agent-driven recast review, owner-dependency mapping, remediation tasks. Then if they sign the brokerage agreement, they're with us for another five to eight months through close.
Stickiness for us is whether the Diagnostic converts to a brokerage agreement, and whether the brokerage closes. 2 of 9 have converted to brokerage already — that's 22% at week 7, trending toward 45% as in-flight engagements close out. Zero closed brokered deals yet. We won't have meaningful close-rate data until month 9 because Main Street deals take six to nine months to close from engagement start.
Q5. What are your unit economics?
Per fully-engaged closed deal: $5K Diagnostic upfront plus 5% of the sale price. On a $1M business that's $55K of revenue. Add the 20% valuation lift our prep work produces, and the deal closes at $1.2M, so the success fee is closer to $60K and total revenue is $65K. Variable cost per engagement today is roughly $400 in AI inference and infrastructure. The rest is founder time, which is what the agent stack is compressing.
The number that matters long-term is deals per operator. Traditional broker shops run 4 to 6 concurrent deals per partner. We're running 4 to 5 today as two founders. The agent stack is built to take that to 20 per operator within 18 months. At 20 deals per operator at $65K average revenue, one operator runs $1.3M of revenue.
Q6. What makes new users try you?
Three reasons.
First, the GAP Report. They get a written valuation across three methods, the buyer archetypes that would actually pay for their business, and a specific 90-day plan to lift the price. That deliverable doesn't exist anywhere else. They want to know what their business is worth before committing to selling it, and we're the only ones who tell them.
Second, the math is obviously better. A traditional broker takes 10% on a $1M sale — $100K — and lists the business with no prep work. We charge $5K upfront and 5% on close — $55K total. The owner pays half the commission and gets a 20% valuation lift on top, so they net materially more even before factoring in the higher close probability.
Third, we put them in front of qualified buyers, not BizBuySell tire-kickers. Our buyer pool is pre-qualified for SBA financing or has documented operating capital — searchers, independent sponsors, ETA buyers, micro-PE funds. We curate outreach by industry and size to the buyers most likely to actually close. Owners selling their life's work want to know who's buying it, and capital-verified buyers close 3 to 4x more reliably than the BizBuySell inquiry pool.
Q7. Why do the reluctant ones hold back?
Two reasons. First, they don't believe AI can do this. They think it's a software toy until they get on the founder call and we walk through their actual numbers. The credibility unlock is technical depth, not marketing.
Second, and this is the harder one — they don't want to know the answer. If we tell them their business isn't saleable in its current shape, that's not financial news, that's identity news. They've spent 20 years building it. Some owners hear that and pull back. We handle this with care, but it's a real friction we can't fully remove. The owners who push through it become our best customers and biggest referrers.
Q8. What are the top things users want?
Three things, in order. They want to know what their business is actually worth — not a vague range, a defensible number. They want to know who would buy it — what kind of buyer, at what price, with what objections. And they want to know what they need to fix before listing — specific, prioritized, with deadlines.
The thing they don't ask for but value most: the SBA pre-qualification. Owners don't know enough to ask. But once we tell them their business has been pre-cleared with Live Oak Bank or another PLP lender, they understand the difference between listing on BizBuySell to whoever inquires and listing to a real, financeable buyer pool. That's the moment the brokerage agreement gets signed.
Q9. What has surprised you about user behavior?
Two things.
One: the "you should not list yet, here's what to fix first" memo strengthens the relationship rather than ending it. We assumed delivering bad news would push owners away. The opposite happened — owners who got that memo expressed gratitude and referred others.
Two: a single CPA referral can produce multiple clients almost immediately. Our three HVAC clients all came from one CPA who saw our work on his first referral and sent us two more within six weeks. That dynamic doesn't exist in seller-direct acquisition — owners sell their business once in a lifetime, so they can't refer many other sellers. But a CPA sees five to ten exit conversations a year. One trusted CPA is structurally worth twenty individual seller referrals.
Q10. What's wrong with existing products in your market?
Five players. Each fails our segment for a different structural reason.
Baton is the closest to us. They do a real diagnostic, they recast financials, they screen sellers aggressively — that's why their close rate is high. But they're a marketplace, so their business model is volume across many small touchpoints. Their AI is a valuation product. They don't actually run the sale process — they hand qualified sellers to a network of human transaction advisors who close the deal. We do the opposite: agents run the entire process, including the closing, and humans approve material actions. They scale by adding advisors. We scale by running more deals per operator.
OffDeal is an AI-native investment bank for businesses with $5 to $100 million in revenue. They've done 30+ transactions, but their economics are built around banker comp — analyst-level work automated, senior bankers running the relationship. That model breaks below $5M revenue because the fees can't support the banker. They aren't coming down to our segment. They're going up.
Iconic is positioned between investment banks and small business brokers, targeting up to $100M revenue. They have a proprietary deal-management platform called Iconic Rail and use AI for buyer sourcing. Their gravity is the lower middle market. A $700K HVAC business doesn't fit their model.
Sunbelt, Murphy, Transworld are the traditional franchises. They run on PDFs, email, and relationship networks built over 40 years. They can't ship an agent stack because they don't have the technical capability and their compensation rewards taking the listing, not closing the deal. They take a 10 to 15% commission and the close rate at their segment is 20 to 30%.
BizBuySell is a listings platform — they explicitly say so. 45,000 listings live, only 2,345 transactions closed in Q1 2026 across the entire country. That gap is the market we're attacking.
The pattern across all of them: nobody owns the prep layer end-to-end with agents. Baton claims 10x advisor capacity through automation. We're targeting 20 because we're not just automating — we're replacing the role.
Q11. What obstacles are in your path?
Three real ones, in order of likelihood.
First, channel velocity. CPAs are the right channel but each partnership takes three months minimum. If we can't get to 20 active CPAs by month 12, we end up competing with Baton on direct seller acquisition and they win because they have more capital. We don't have a clever fix — it's a grind. The unlock is the white-label ExitIQ product, which gives CPAs a reason to integrate beyond just referrals.
Second, close-rate translation. Our thesis is that the prep work raises close rate from the 20% Main Street baseline to something materially higher. We won't know if that's true until deal 50. If certified deals close at 25% — only modestly better than baseline — the certification doesn't have enough lift to justify lender subscriptions or CPA licenses. We've built explicit measurement and a pivot trigger at deal 50.
Third, an incumbent moving down-market. Iconic is the most plausible candidate — tech-enabled M&A advisory, already AI-fluent, currently focused on $100M revenue businesses. If they decide to add a Main Street prep layer in 18 months, they have brand and capital advantages. Our mitigation is speed and dataset lead. By the time they could enter, we want 200 paid engagements worth of deal-process data and lender pre-qualification relationships they'd have to displace, not just compete with.
Q12. How big can this be?
Three layers, each larger than the last.
Layer one — broker replacement. We replace the traditional small business broker in our segment. Sub-$2M Main Street is roughly 150,000 transactions a year nationally. At our economics, $65K revenue per closed deal, the broker-replacement opportunity alone is roughly $10B annually. That's the floor of the business — just being the broker, but better, at scale.
Layer two — Scorta Certification as the industry standard. The Scorta Score becomes the certification SBA lenders, CPAs, search funders, and family offices reference by name when assessing Main Street businesses. Lenders subscribe for certified deal flow. CPAs license per-seat to run the Diagnostic on their books. Buyers pay for access to certified inventory. This is the FICO move — own the protocol, not just the broker function. The Scorta Score gets cited in pre-qualification checklists, listing pages, and deal data rooms. We license the methodology to other brokers who pay us to certify their listings. Sunbelt and Transworld franchisees become Scorta-certified resellers. At that point we take a take rate on every transaction in the system, whether we originated it or not.
Layer three — running the transaction rails. We move past brokerage into the infrastructure underneath every Main Street deal. Closing escrow. Transaction insurance. Seller financing facilitation. Lender marketplace embedded inside Scorta. The Lender Ops Agent already operates inside lender portals and VDRs. The natural extension is owning the closing flow itself. Once we're inside thousands of closes annually, we become the rails the entire small business M&A market runs on. Buyers and sellers and lenders all transact through Scorta because Scorta is the system of record. That's the FICO-plus-Visa outcome — the standard and the network.
Five years from now, the company is predominantly software and certification fees, not concierge brokerage labor. The brokerage is the wedge that gets us inside enough deals to own the protocol. Once the protocol is established, the protocol does the work.
