**Overall verdict from the red team**

The plan is tighter than prior versions but still does not survive contact with reality. It is built on a file the company refuses to collect, a buyer gate brokers will not install, a competitor that already shipped the core wedge, and an 11-week org chart for a two-founder brokerage that is about to be shut down.

### Single assumption that collapses the entire thesis
Brokers will put a Plaid / proof-of-funds / citizenship wall in front of the CIM, enough buyers will finish it, and the resulting record becomes a portable standard. If that is false (and the panel believes it is), Instant Qualification becomes a lead tax, the Passport never seeds, $129/listing has no engine, “the pair” is a slide, and Demo Day is just a broker-SaaS company with no retention.

### The 7 Critical issues

1. **Day-one underwriting without the file**  
   You claim to know financeability + buyer + structure on day one, yet Architecture Rule 1 forbids seller financials in every product sold in weeks 1–5. Public listing SDE is marketing copy. You cannot score “the pair” without both sides of the file.

2. **Fannie Mae without Fannie Mae**  
   Desktop Underwriter became standard because GSEs required it. 7(a) has no equivalent mandate. Calling the format the moat before anyone is forced to run it is classic “infrastructure for X” failure.

3. **Competitive table is already false**  
   LoanBud launched pre-listing SBA prequalification + broker workspace + buyer apply-into-50+ lenders in Feb 2026. BizBuySell already has the SBA Loan Eligible badge and iBusiness in the Finance Center. The “Them vs Scorta” table describes 2024.

4. **Plaid-before-CIM is a lead-kill**  
   Gating the CIM behind KYC + Plaid + eligibility is not a byproduct — it is a wall. Brokers currently do NDA → PDF. A 40% completion “pass” means 60% of inquiries (including the real buyer) die. Brokers will bypass you.

5. **$129/listing sold to people who will not pay**  
   Showcase/Diamond brokers already convert and will not install a startup in the inquiry path. Stale 90-day listings do not generate the volume Instant Qualification needs. Per-listing pricing is a tax on mostly dead inventory.

6. **$55k MRR + 10 pods + brokerage offline = a company you do not have**  
   Two founders currently write all the code and the only real cash comes from brokerage. The plan staffs 10 engineers, shuts the working revenue, and tries to sell SaaS in 11 weeks to the people it previously said it would replace.

7. **Auto-Passport + ranked inbox is an unlicensed CRA**  
   Persisting identity + liquidity + citizenship/SBA eligibility and ranking buyers for brokers is a consumer report under FCRA. Calling it a “verdict card” does not change that. One ranked-out buyer creates statutory risk that ends the batch.

### High-severity issues (condensed)
- Bankable + lender rail creates the two-master problem under 13 CFR § 103.4(g).
- Kill tests are late, lagging, and easy to rewrite (especially reprice-to-close in 3 weeks and the 40% completion bar).
- Four paid listings is not conservative — it is the entire model. Reality is closer to 1.0–1.5.
- $850 “Bankable” is a findings memo, not something a lender will rely on (and the company forbade itself from using verification language).
- The long-term ladder (escrow, seller notes, insurance) is mostly other companies’ licensed businesses.

### Most likely kill paths
- **Next 11 weeks**: Brokers refuse the inquiry gate → completion stays low or you gut the product → one office reports lost leads → outbound stalls → $55k becomes theater on trials → brokerage is already offline so there is no cash fallback.
- **12–24 months**: BizBuySell + LoanBud absorb the listing-side and buyer-apply-on-this-deal products. You are either an unlicensed CRA or a low-retention per-listing tool with no mandate. The ladder never starts.

The panel’s bottom line: the plan already names most of these risks, then treats each as mitigated by the unproven product. That is circular. The week-6 branch is the only fully honest page — and it admits the company does not yet know which business it actually is.
